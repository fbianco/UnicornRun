A ROAD MADE OF SLICES

seen from above: equal slices

projected: trapezoids that shrink with distance

scale = depth ÷ z
x′ = centre + scale · x · k
y′ = horizon − scale · y · k

1,290 slices, each 200 units deep, each with a curve value and a height.

Curves: every slice is shifted sideways by an amount that keeps growing (dx += curve).

Hills: slices simply sit higher or lower.

This is the classic technique of 16-bit and arcade racers, and it is why the game needs no 3D engine. The track is not a 3D model: it is a list of 1,290 thin slices. Each slice knows how much the road bends there and how high it is. For the camera behind each unicorn, we project the two edges of every slice ahead with the three formulas on the right: things twice as far away are drawn half as big. Curves are a cheat: we never rotate anything, we just slide each slice a little further sideways than the previous one, and the eye reads it as a bend.
