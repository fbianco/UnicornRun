JUMP ON THE LINE

Warning: 2.5 s before a hurdle, HURDLE AHEAD! and a filling bar.

The line is painted where you take off: a time, not a distance, before the hurdle. 0.75 s for bodies, 0.45 s for keys.

Why time: any take-off 0.05 to 0.7 s before the hurdle clears it, at any speed. A body jump is seen about 0.3 s late.

Tested at trot, half and full speed, with the jump seen 0.15 to 0.45 s late.

Children saw the hurdles, but jumped too late or too early. The jump itself is physics: a unicorn that takes off anywhere between five hundredths of a second and seven tenths of a second before the hurdle flies over it. That window is a time, so at full speed it is a long stretch of road and at a trot a short one. So the striped line is not painted at a fixed place: it sits 0.75 seconds of running before the hurdle and slides with your speed. Body players get it earlier than keyboard players because a real jump, with the crouch and the camera, is seen about a third of a second after the child starts it. A test runs the unicorn at three speeds and checks that jumping on the line always clears the hurdle.
